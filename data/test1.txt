Thomas came by around nine, carrying a bag of fresh bread from the bakery in town. "Heard there was some trouble out on the water last night," he said, setting the bag on her table. "A boat radioed in from Millbrook, said they got turned around in the fog but made it back safe."

"That's good," Margaret said, relief settling into her shoulders. "I was worried."

"You're always worried," Thomas said, though not unkindly. "It's part of what makes you good at this job."

She poured him a cup of coffee without being asked; they'd known each other long enough that certain things didn't need asking anymore. Outside, the tide was coming in, slow and rhythmic, the way it had for longer than either of them had been alive, and would continue long after they were both gone.

"Do you ever think about retiring?" Thomas asked after a while, his voice softer than usual.

Margaret considered the question longer than she expected to. "Sometimes," she admitted. "On the bad nights, mostly. When my knees hurt and the wind won't stop howling and I can't remember why I chose this life in the first place."

"And on the good nights?"

"On the good nights, I remember exactly why." She looked out toward the water, toward the horizon where the sky met the sea in a line so straight it almost didn't look real. "There's nothing else I'd rather be doing. Not one thing."

Thomas nodded slowly, as if he understood completely, which perhaps he did. He'd spent his whole life by this same water, after all, pulling traps and mending nets and watching the same tides rise and fall a thousand times over. Some people were built for the ocean, and some weren't, and there wasn't much use pretending otherwise.

That evening, as the sun began to dip toward the horizon, Margaret climbed the two hundred and thirty-seven steps once more, her legs steady despite the sleepless night before. She lit the lamp exactly as she had every evening for eleven years, and watched as its beam swept out across the darkening water, patient and unwavering, a promise kept one more time.
